# Research: Can a GCash payment QR be custom-themed safely? (2026)

All sources accessed **2026-09-22**.

## 1. Question & decision it serves

**Question:** Can we safely re-render a GCash payment QR in a custom theme (Crates N' Plates brand colors, logo in center, styled frame), or must the official GCash QR image be used untouched?

**Decision it serves:** pick one of —

- **(a)** full themed server-side QR render via a PHP library (decode payload → re-encode with brand styling),
- **(b)** official GCash QR image placed inside a branded frame only,
- **(c)** plain admin upload of the official QR image.

## 2. How GCash QR payment actually works

### Payload format: QR Ph = EMVCo Merchant-Presented Mode

- GCash is a BSP-supervised EMI; BSP Circular No. 1055 (2019) mandates adoption of a **National QR Code Standard**, to be "aligned with the globally-recognized standard", approved by the PSMB (PPMI in the Philippines) [1]; that standard is **QR Ph** [2], which BSP describes as *"Aligned with the Europay-Mastercard VISA Co (EMVCo) standard, the global standard for secure payments"* [3].
- BSP's P2M FAQ spells out why: *"QR Ph thus allows a quick, low-cost and safe method to pay, transfer to and receive funds from other bank and e-money accounts in the Philippines"* — i.e., one interoperable national code, interpreted by any participating bank or EMI [3].
- Therefore GCash merchant QRs are **QR Ph P2M codes = EMVCo QR Code Specification, Merchant-Presented Mode (EMV® QRCPS MPM)** payload structures: an EMVCo TLV (tag-length-value) string — Payload Format Indicator `00`, Point of Initiation `01` (`11` static / `12` dynamic), Merchant Account Information template containing a Globally Unique Identifier (GUI) naming the payment system, amounts/currency (ids `53`/`54`) for dynamic codes, terminated by CRC16-CCITT-FALSE id `63` — encoded as a standard **ISO/IEC 18004 QR Code symbol** [4][5].
  - Spec lineage note: EMV QRCPS normatively references **ISO/IEC 18004** (the QR symbology spec). ISO/IEC 18092 is the NFC protocol (NFCIP-1), not QR [13]; see §6.
- From BSP M-2023-005: since **1 July 2023** all proprietary (non-QR-Ph) payment QR codes in the Philippines are **disabled**; sending PSP apps "shall no longer support the scanning of non-QR Ph codes"; receiving PSPs must "require such merchants/businesses to **display and utilize the QR Ph codes** in their payment acceptance" [2]. So a GCash merchant QR is a QR Ph P2M EMVCo payload — not a proprietary GCash format and not a URL.

### Static vs dynamic; merchant QR vs personal QR

- **Static merchant QR** (`01`=`11`): no amount in payload; the payer types the amount after scanning. **Dynamic** (`01`=`12`): amount pre-embedded [4][5]. GCash's help flow ("Check the payment details, then tap Pay", amount prompt when applicable) is consistent with merchant-presented static QR Ph [6].
- **Merchant QR**: generated by GCash for the merchant ("merchants only need to print the QR code that their respective payment service providers will generate for them, display those codes" [3]); approved merchants receive a GCash **QR Kit** (QR standee, notification phone, door hanger) after "QR production and kitting" [7].
- **Personal QR / P2P QR**: per GCash Terms, *"'Personal QR,' 'P2P QR,' or 'Transfer QR' means a unique QR code associated with a GCash Account that facilitates peer-to-peer (P2P) or send-money transactions"* — used via Express Send: "you can **scan or upload** a Personal QR" [8].
- **Customer-presented mode** also exists: consumer taps "Generate QR → Pay using QR Code" and the merchant scans it [6]; EMVCo maintains separate CPM and MPM specifications [9].

### What the GCash app does on scan

Per GCash help [6]: Tap **QR** → **Scan the merchant's QR code** → **Check the payment details** → **Pay**; both parties get SMS confirmation. The BSP describes the generic flow as "Scan the merchant-recipient's QR Ph code and if prompted by the application, type the amount… Check the details and approve" [3].

Mechanically (per ISO/IEC 18004 decode procedure and EMV QRCPS):

1. The camera image is reduced to a binary module matrix via a Global Threshold computed **from the image's own max/min reflectance** — i.e., the decoder binarizes on relative contrast, not on absolute black/white colors [11 §12, step x].
2. Format info → EC level + mask (invalid format bits trigger a mirror-image decode attempt [11 §12, step u]); finder/timing/alignment patterns locate and resample the grid; codewords are read, de-interleaved, Reed-Solomon **error-corrected** (§7.5 / Annex B), and the original message string is restored [11 §12, §7.5].
3. EMVCo: *"The EMV QR Code Specifications address the QR Code itself and the way the data is coded into the QR code. The specific messaging after reading and processing are left to the payment providers."* The app parses the TLV string (scheme identified by GUI), validates the CRC, and initiates the payment through GCash backend rails [9][4]. A generic QR reader can decode the string but cannot complete the payment — a payment app is required [9].

**Consequence:** the app cares only that the decoded string is byte-identical. There is no signature, watermark, or provenance check binding the *image* to the payload: the payload's integrity check (CRC id `63`) is computed over the TLV string, not over any rendering of it [4][5]. GCash/BSP documentation further shows QR images travel as ordinary images: users "send a screenshot or image of the QR code" and the receiver "can scan or **upload** the QR code" [3][8] — the app accepts QR pixels from arbitrary sources by design.

## 3. What breaks a GCash scan

All limits below are from ISO/IEC 18004:2015 (QR symbology spec; current edition is ISO/IEC 18004:2024 [12]) and EMVCo-derived payments guidance, not from blogs.

| Factor | Spec requirement / limit | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Quiet zone | Minimum **4X (4 modules) on all four sides** for QR Code symbols; accompanying text "should not interfere with the symbol itself nor the quiet zones" | ISO/IEC 18004 §6.3.8, §9.1, §9.2 [11] |
| Error correction | Reed-Solomon; four levels, approx. damage recovery: **L 7% / M 15% / Q 25% / H 30%** of codewords (worked example: version 6-H corrects up to ~32.6% as substitution errors) | ISO/IEC 18004 §7.5.1 Table 8 [11] |
| Erasure clustering penalty | `e + 2t ≤ d`; when erasures exceed half the EC codewords, misdecode-protection `p = 3` — **clustered damage (a logo) recovers less than the headline %** | ISO/IEC 18004 §7.5.1 [11] |
| Function patterns | Finder/timing/alignment patterns and format-info modules are not protected like data codewords; they are graded separately as **Fixed Pattern Damage**, and the overall scan grade is the *lowest* of symbol contrast, modulation, fixed pattern damage, decode, axial/grid non-uniformity and unused-EC grades — one damaged finder tanks the grade | ISO/IEC 18004 §10.2.1, §10.2.2, Annex G [11] |
| Contrast / polarity | Decoder binarizes at a Global Threshold derived from the image itself (§12); the standard explicitly supports **reflectance reversal — "Symbols are intended to be read when marked so that the image is either dark on light or light on dark"** (§6.2) and mirror-image decoding (§12) — so inverted or colored renderings are spec-legal **provided sufficient light/dark contrast remains** | ISO/IEC 18004 §6.2, §12 [11] |
| Contrast (print/display quality) | Verify symbol in final configuration; glossy/specular surfaces change scanning performance; matt surfaces help; illumination must "optimize the desired contrast components" | ISO/IEC 18004 Annex K.1 [11] |
| EC level choice | Q = "suitable for more critical or poor print quality applications", H = maximum reliability; AusPayNet's EMVCo-derived MPM guidance: keep QRs uncrowded, test on a representative range of devices, mind paper type (print) and **contrast ratio + resolution (digital displays)**, "Error level L is recommended" for size reasons | ISO/IEC 18004 Annex K.2; AusPayNet MPM QR spec §9 "Merchant QR Code Readability" [11][5] |
| Logo/logo placement | EMVCo-derived payments branding guideline: logos belong **on the frame**; *"The QR should not contain any logo of the Payment System inside the QR itself"*; payee/institution logos may appear on the QR frame | AusPayNet Industry Standard MPM QR, Ch. 9 Branding Guideline [5] |
| GCash's own troubleshooting | "Make sure the QR code is clear"; QR must fit the in-app scan box; clean lens | GCash Help [10] |

**Logo coverage limit:** no publicly accessible primary source states a normative "logo may cover X% of the QR" figure — the only EMVCo document that would (Merchant-Presented QR Guidance and Examples) requires an EMVCo login [14] → [UNVERIFIED]. The spec-derived engineering bound: a center logo must stay (i) clear of finder/timing/alignment patterns and the quiet zone, and (ii) within the chosen EC level's recovery capacity **minus the erasure-clustering penalty** — i.e., well under the H-level ≈30% codeword ceiling. Rendering at EC level **H** and keeping the logo in the middle of the data region at a small fraction of the symbol area keeps damage inside recoverable bounds per §7.5.1 [11].

## 4. Re-encoding safety: technical and legal

### Technical: is decoding payload → re-render safe?

**Yes, with spec-conformant rendering.** Because (i) the app validates only the decoded TLV string (CRC over the string, not the image) [4][5], (ii) ISO/IEC 18004 defines exactly the tolerances a re-render must satisfy (quiet zone 4X, contrast via relative threshold, EC capacity, function-pattern integrity) [11], and (iii) GCash/BSP flows explicitly accept QR **images** from anywhere — screenshots, uploads [3][8] — a re-encoded symbol carrying the byte-identical payload behaves identically to the official image in the GCash app. Requirements for the themed render to keep scanning: EC level H; ≥4-module quiet zone kept clear; high light/dark contrast (dark-on-light safest; inversion is spec-legal but untested against GCash's app [UNVERIFIED empirically]); finder/timing/alignment areas untouched; logo confined to data region well inside EC capacity; sufficient on-screen/print module size.

### Legal / GCash ToS: is altering the QR image allowed?

No clause found in GCash's public Terms and Conditions that explicitly forbids restyling the QR *image* — but three clauses create real, unquantified risk [8]:

- **AUP §1.2:** *"Alter, modify, or cause the alteration or modification of the GCash Services. You further agree not to use the GCash App and GCash Services for any commercial or business use, without prior written authorization from GCash."*
- **AUP §1.11:** agree not to *"modify, lease, loan, sell, distribute, or create derivative works based on any content, material, or information available on or through the GCash App and GCash Services unless you have been specifically permitted in writing to do so by GCash…"* — a QR image obtained from the GCash app/portal is plausibly "content available on or through the GCash App and GCash Services"; a themed re-render is plausibly a "derivative work".
- **AUP §1.1 / §3.2:** no confusing use of GCash's or third parties' trademarks/logos; no removing/altering/obscuring proprietary-rights notices accompanying GCash-partner products/services. (Relevant if the official QR artwork carries GCash/EMV marks, and if we put a GCash logo in our themed center.)

Regulatory layer:

- BSP M-2023-005 ¶2 requires PSPs to make merchants "**display and utilize the QR Ph codes in their payment acceptance**"; that obligation flows into merchant agreements (the GCash merchant MOA is not public) — an altered QR could be treated by GCash as non-display of the official code [2].
- BSP Circular 1055 §802(7)(a): the PSMB maintains *"National QR Code specifications, such as the **prescribed structure and design** of merchant-presented and customer-presented QR"* — a non-public PPMI document that may prescribe visual design, not just payload [1]. Its contents are [UNVERIFIED].
- EMV QR Marks (QR Payment Mark / QR Scan Icon) require EMVCo's royalty-free trademark license to reproduce [9]; GCash marks require GCash permission. A themed render that *drops* those marks and uses only our own brand avoids mark-licensing issues but may conflict with QR Ph display requirements [UNVERIFIED].

**Bottom line:** technically the payload doesn't care how it is drawn; the exposure on option (a) is contractual/regulatory, not technical, and it cannot be cleared from public primary sources — only from written GCash confirmation.

## 5. Verdict

| Option | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Full themed render** (decode → re-encode with brand colors/logo) | **Technically sound, legally unverifiable → not recommended without written GCash sign-off** | Technically: payload-identical re-render satisfies ISO/IEC 18004 tolerances (EC H, 4X quiet zone, contrast, function patterns intact) and the app only checks the decoded string [11][4][5][3][8]. Legally: AUP §§1.2/1.11 derivative-work & alteration clauses, M-2023-005 display obligation, and the non-public "prescribed structure and design" spec are unresolved [8][2][1] → ToS-safety [UNVERIFIED] |
| **(b) Official QR inside a branded frame** | **RECOMMENDED** | The official image is untouched (no payload, derivative-work, or "display the QR Ph code" exposure); all branding sits on the **frame**, which is exactly where EMVCo-derived branding guidance puts it — logos on the frame, nothing inside the symbol [5]; ISO/IEC 18004 §9.2 requires only that surrounding content not interfere with the symbol or quiet zone — keep a ≥4-module clear band around the QR and adequate contrast [11]. Delivers the branded look with zero scan risk |
| **(c) Plain upload of official QR image** | **Zero-risk fallback** | No alteration of any kind; trivially complies with every source above; matches GCash's own QR Kit / screenshot-sharing model [7][3] |

**Practical recommendation:** ship **(b)** — mount the official GCash QR image inside a Crates N' Plates frame (brand color background outside a ≥4-module white quiet-zone band, logo/copy only in the frame margin), served at sufficient resolution. Keep **(c)** as the admin fallback. Pursue **(a)** only if GCash grants written authorization; if ever pursued, render with EC level H, inverted-contrast forbidden pending app testing, and logo strictly inside the data region below the §7.5.1 recovery bound [11].

## 6. Not verified ([UNVERIFIED])

1. **PPMI National QR Code "structure and design" document contents** — Circular 1055 §802(7)(a) confirms such a document exists but it is maintained privately by PPMI and made available only to BSP on request; whether it prescribes merchant-QR visual design (colors, logo, frame) is unknown [1].
2. **GCash merchant MOA / merchant terms** — actual contract language on displaying or altering the official QR is not public [7][2].
3. **Any explicit GCash ToS clause on altering the QR *image*** — none found in the public Terms; AUP §§1.2/1.11 are adjacent but not image-specific [8].
4. **EMVCo "Merchant-Presented QR Guidance and Examples" specifics** (e.g., a normative logo-coverage percentage) — the document exists behind EMVCo login; content not readable [14].
5. **The exact GUI string GCash embeds in its Merchant Account Information TLV** — QR Ph = EMVCo is confirmed [1][3], but the payload of a live GCash QR was not decoded in this research.
6. **Empirical scan testing** — no test performed of themed renders (any color/logo variant) against the real GCash app; spec analysis predicts H-level themed renders with 4X quiet zone will scan, but this is inference from the spec, not an observation.
7. **Whether GCash's official QR image contains baked-in GCash/EMV logo artwork** inside the symbol — depends on the actual asset; check the production asset before framing (removing/altering proprietary notices would engage AUP §3.2 [8]).
8. **Spec lineage correction:** the brief referenced "ISO/IEC 18092 & 17022". ISO/IEC 18092 is NFC (NFCIP-1) [13], not QR; ISO/IEC 17022 does not exist as an ISO/IEC QR standard (nearest match ISO/TS 17022, an audit-report standard). The QR-relevant pair is **ISO/IEC 18004** (symbology) + **ISO/IEC 15415** (2D print quality), the latter being ISO 18004's normative print-quality reference [11]. Version note: clause numbers/tables above are ISO/IEC 18004:2015 (3rd ed.); ISO/IEC 18004:2024 is the current edition [12].
